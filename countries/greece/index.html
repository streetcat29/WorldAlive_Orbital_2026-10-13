How Greece's population is changing

Greece's population is roughly stable: +0.08% a year, which works out to about +8,832 people per year, or +24 a day. Births (6.6 per 1,000) and deaths (12.1 per 1,000) alone would give −0.55% a year; net migration adds about 16,636 people a year (+0.16%). Roughly one in every 789 people on Earth lives in Greece.

Population of Greece since 1960

In 1960, Greece had 8,331,725 people. By 2025 that was 10,413,962 — 1.2× larger. The population peaked in 2010 at 11,121,341 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1974 and 1984, when the population rose by 10%.

Greece from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Greece at about 2,250,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 9,938,847 in 2025 (4× 1800) and projects 6,275,570 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2010 at 11,124,666 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
