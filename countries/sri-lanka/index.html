How Sri Lanka's population is changing

Sri Lanka's population is shrinking: −0.73% a year, which works out to about −159,415 people per year, or −436 a day. Births (10.1 per 1,000) and deaths (7.8 per 1,000) alone would give +0.23% a year; net migration removes about 30,724 people a year (−0.14%). Roughly one in every 378 people on Earth lives in Sri Lanka.

Population of Sri Lanka since 1960

In 1960, Sri Lanka had 9,661,389 people. By 2025 that was 21,756,000 — 2.3× larger. The population peaked in 2022 at 22,181,000 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1960 and 1970, when the population rose by 27%.

Sri Lanka from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Sri Lanka at about 1,750,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 23,229,474 in 2025 (13× 1800) and projects 21,249,404 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2051 at 24,814,756 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
