How Mexico's population is changing

Mexico's population is growing: +0.83% a year, which works out to about +1,090,386 people per year, or +2,985 a day. Births (15.5 per 1,000) and deaths (6.2 per 1,000) alone would give +0.92% a year; net migration removes about 108,037 people a year (−0.08%). Roughly one in every 62 people on Earth lives in Mexico.

Mexico from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Mexico at about 5,100,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 131,946,897 in 2025 (26× 1800) and projects 130,248,835 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2058 at 149,937,276 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
