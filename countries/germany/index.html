How Germany's population is changing

Germany's population is roughly stable: −0.03% a year, which works out to about −25,340 people per year, or −69 a day. Births (8.1 per 1,000) and deaths (12.1 per 1,000) alone would give −0.40% a year; net migration removes about 334,072 people a year (−0.40%). Roughly one in every 98 people on Earth lives in Germany.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.

Read this country carefully

A small annual change can hide large opposing flows

The bundled 2025 population-growth rate for Germany is close to zero. That does not mean births, deaths or migration stopped; a net rate can be small when larger components offset one another.

The indicators on this page are not all from the same reference year. Do not assign the net change to one cause by subtracting mismatched-year values. Compare the historical population series first, then inspect aligned official flow data if you need an explanation. See the accounting distinction.
