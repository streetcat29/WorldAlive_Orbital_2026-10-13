How Russia's population is changing

Russia's population is roughly stable: −0.11% a year, which works out to about −156,235 people per year, or −428 a day. Births (8.4 per 1,000) and deaths (12.4 per 1,000) alone would give −0.40% a year; net migration removes about 251,822 people a year (−0.18%). Roughly one in every 57 people on Earth lives in Russia.

Population of Russia since 1960

In 1960, Russia had 119,897,000 people. By 2025 that was 143,513,328 — 1.2× larger. The population peaked in 1992 at 148,538,197 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1960 and 1970, when the population rose by 9%.

Russia from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Russia at about 31,295,641 people in 1800; the UN estimates 143,997,393 in 2025 (5× 1800) and projects 126,386,520 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 1991 at 149,448,433 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
