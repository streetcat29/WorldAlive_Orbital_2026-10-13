How Puerto Rico's population is changing

Puerto Rico's population is shrinking: −0.55% a year, which works out to about −17,637 people per year, or −48 a day. Births (5.7 per 1,000) and deaths (10.3 per 1,000) alone would give −0.46% a year; net migration adds about 5,495 people a year (+0.17%). Roughly one in every 2,580 people on Earth lives in Puerto Rico.

Population of Puerto Rico since 1960

In 1960, Puerto Rico had 2,358,000 people. By 2025 that was 3,184,835 — 1.4× larger. The population peaked in 2004 at 3,826,878 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1969 and 1979, when the population rose by 18%.

Puerto Rico from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Puerto Rico at about 146,063 people in 1800; the UN estimates 3,235,290 in 2025 (22× 1800) and projects 995,622 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2001 at 3,813,009 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
