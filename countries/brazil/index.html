How Brazil's population is changing

Brazil's population is growing: +0.38% a year, which works out to about +815,392 people per year, or +2,232 a day. Births (12.1 per 1,000) and deaths (7.1 per 1,000) alone would give +0.51% a year; net migration removes about 217,283 people a year (−0.10%). Roughly one in every 39 people on Earth lives in Brazil.

Brazil from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Brazil at about 2,500,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 212,812,397 in 2025 (85× 1800) and projects 163,364,063 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2042 at 219,319,762 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
