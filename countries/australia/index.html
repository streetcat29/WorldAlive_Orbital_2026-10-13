How Australia's population is changing

Australia's population is growing fast: +1.53% a year, which works out to about +423,354 people per year, or +1,159 a day. Births (10.7 per 1,000) and deaths (6.9 per 1,000) alone would give +0.38% a year; net migration adds about 137,109 people a year (+0.50%). Roughly one in every 298 people on Earth lives in Australia.

Australia from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Australia at about 200,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 26,974,024 in 2025 (135× 1800) and projects 43,143,685 for 2100 in its medium scenario. That is still 60% growth over the rest of this century.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
