How China's population is changing

China's population is roughly stable: −0.17% a year, which works out to about −2,387,972 people per year, or −6,538 a day. Births (6.8 per 1,000) and deaths (7.8 per 1,000) alone would give −0.10% a year; net migration removes about 268,126 people a year (−0.02%). Roughly one in every 6 people on Earth lives in China.

China from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put China at about 330,000,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 1,416,096,093 in 2025 (4× 1800) and projects 633,368,105 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2021 at 1,426,437,269 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
