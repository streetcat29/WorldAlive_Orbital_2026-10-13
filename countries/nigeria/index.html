How Nigeria's population is changing

Nigeria's population is growing fast: +2.06% a year, which works out to about +4,898,468 people per year, or +13,412 a day. Births (32.5 per 1,000) and deaths (11.6 per 1,000) alone would give +2.09% a year; net migration removes about 15,258 people a year (−0.01%). Roughly one in every 35 people on Earth lives in Nigeria.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.

Read this country carefully

A faster rate is not a guaranteed future population

Nigeria's positive annual growth estimate describes change over a reference period. Extending the same percentage far into the future is a constant-rate scenario—not a UN forecast and not evidence that the rate will persist.

The page's long-run UN series uses demographic assumptions that can change over time. Compare values within that series rather than joining a World Bank estimate to a different UN vintage and calling the difference observed growth. Understand the two kinds of projection.
