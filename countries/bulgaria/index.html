How Bulgaria's population is changing

Bulgaria's population is roughly stable: −0.13% a year, which works out to about −8,114 people per year, or −22 a day. Births (8.3 per 1,000) and deaths (15.6 per 1,000) alone would give −0.73% a year; net migration removes about 2,961 people a year (−0.05%). Roughly one in every 1,277 people on Earth lives in Bulgaria.

Population of Bulgaria since 1960

In 1960, Bulgaria had 7,867,374 people. By 2025 that was 6,433,302 — 18% smaller. The population peaked in 1988 at 8,981,446 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1960 and 1970, when the population rose by 8%.

Bulgaria from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Bulgaria at about 2,250,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 6,714,556 in 2025 (3× 1800) and projects 3,531,199 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 1988 at 8,985,721 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
