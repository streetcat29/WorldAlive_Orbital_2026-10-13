How Italy's population is changing

Italy's population is roughly stable: −0.06% a year, which works out to about −37,036 people per year, or −101 a day. Births (6.3 per 1,000) and deaths (11.0 per 1,000) alone would give −0.47% a year; net migration adds about 75,448 people a year (+0.13%). Roughly one in every 139 people on Earth lives in Italy.

Population of Italy since 1960

In 1960, Italy had 50,199,700 people. By 2025 that was 58,915,656 — 1.2× larger. The population peaked in 2014 at 60,320,707 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1960 and 1970, when the population rose by 7%.

Italy from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Italy at about 19,000,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 59,146,263 in 2025 (3× 1800) and projects 35,376,820 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2014 at 60,664,169 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
