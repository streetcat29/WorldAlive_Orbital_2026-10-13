How Cuba's population is changing

Cuba's population is shrinking: −0.39% a year, which works out to about −42,497 people per year, or −116 a day. Births (8.6 per 1,000) and deaths (10.4 per 1,000) alone would give −0.18% a year; net migration removes about 22,244 people a year (−0.20%). Roughly one in every 751 people on Earth lives in Cuba.

Population of Cuba since 1960

In 1960, Cuba had 7,238,081 people. By 2025 that was 10,937,203 — 1.5× larger. The population peaked in 2012 at 11,303,175 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1960 and 1970, when the population rose by 22%.

Cuba from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Cuba at about 272,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 10,937,203 in 2025 (40× 1800) and projects 5,577,282 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2012 at 11,303,172 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
