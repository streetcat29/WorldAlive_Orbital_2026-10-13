How Japan's population is changing

Japan's population is shrinking: −0.49% a year, which works out to about −607,141 people per year, or −1,662 a day. Births (5.7 per 1,000) and deaths (13.3 per 1,000) alone would give −0.76% a year; net migration adds about 140,579 people a year (+0.11%). Roughly one in every 67 people on Earth lives in Japan.

Population of Japan since 1960

In 1960, Japan had 93,216,000 people. By 2025 that was 123,366,734 — 1.3× larger. The population peaked in 2010 at 128,070,000 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1966 and 1976, when the population rose by 14%.

Japan from 1800 to 2100

About these numbers

The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.

Read this country carefully

An older population is not an individual life forecast

Japan's age distribution and its life-expectancy figure answer different questions. The share aged 65+ describes the people in the population in the stated year; life expectancy at birth summarizes period mortality conditions for a hypothetical newborn.

Subtracting your age from that figure does not estimate how long you have left to live. Nor does a higher elderly share by itself establish why population is falling. Read a worked age-structure example, then compare the definitions and years here.
