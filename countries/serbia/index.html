How Serbia's population is changing

Serbia's population is shrinking: −0.57% a year, which works out to about −37,227 people per year, or −102 a day. Births (9.2 per 1,000) and deaths (14.9 per 1,000) alone would give −0.57% a year; net migration removes about 13,086 people a year (−0.20%). Roughly one in every 1,254 people on Earth lives in Serbia.

Population of Serbia since 1960

In 1960, Serbia had 6,638,992 people. By 2025 that was 6,549,143 — almost unchanged. The population peaked in 1990 at 7,897,937 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1960 and 1970, when the population rose by 8%.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
