How Poland's population is changing

Poland's population is shrinking: −0.34% a year, which works out to about −123,164 people per year, or −337 a day. Births (6.9 per 1,000) and deaths (11.2 per 1,000) alone would give −0.43% a year; net migration removes about 330,820 people a year (−0.91%). Roughly one in every 225 people on Earth lives in Poland.

Population of Poland since 1960

In 1960, Poland had 29,637,450 people. By 2025 that was 36,435,861 — 1.2× larger. The population peaked in 1998 at 38,663,481 and has declined since. The fastest growth came between 1960 and 1970, when the population rose by 10%.

Poland from 1800 to 2100

Modelled estimates put Poland at about 9,000,000 people in 1800; the UN estimates 38,140,913 in 2025 (4× 1800) and projects 19,329,843 for 2100 in its medium scenario. The population is projected to peak around 2023 at 38,762,847 and decline afterwards.

About these numbers

Population, growth, birth and death rates, migration, density, age structure, life expectancy, fertility and GDP per capita come from the World Bank Indicators API (latest available year shown beside each figure). The live counter applies the annual growth rate continuously from 1 July 2025 — a model, not a measurement. City populations are the latest census or official estimate held by GeoNames and are not projected. Sub-city units are folded into their parent city.
