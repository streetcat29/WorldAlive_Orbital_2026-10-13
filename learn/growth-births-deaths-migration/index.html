Population growth is not births minus deaths alone

Separate stocks, rates and flows before explaining a change. Türkiye's local records show why a plausible calculation may still be the wrong accounting exercise.

Begin with the accounting identity

For a defined population over a defined interval, population change equals births minus deaths plus immigration minus emigration, with any statistical adjustments stated separately. A published series may include revisions or boundary changes. The identity is useful only when its pieces cover the same people and period. Subtracting numbers that happen to be available is not enough.

Population is a stock measured at a reference time. Births, deaths and migration are flows over a period. A crude birth rate is births per thousand people per year, not a percentage growth rate and not a count. A net migration value is a signed count, not the number of immigrants. Positive and negative flows must be kept in their own units until converted deliberately. The birth-rate denominator is the estimated midyear population, not the number of potential mothers. This country-period measure is not an individual annual probability.

A same-series change you can reproduce

The bundled World Bank history for Türkiye records 85,518,661 people in 2024 and 85,878,556 in 2025. The difference is 85,878,556 − 85,518,661 = 359,895. As a simple percentage of the earlier stock, 359,895 ÷ 85,518,661 × 100 = 0.4208%. This is a change between annual reference populations, not necessarily a calendar-year count of registered events.

The bundle's 2025 growth indicator is 0.4200%. It is close to, but not exactly, that simple percentage. Using a continuous-rate convention, ln(85,878,556 ÷ 85,518,661) × 100 = 0.4200%. This reproduces the growth indicator to the shown precision. A small difference between a log rate and an ordinary percentage is a formula distinction, not evidence of missing people.

The tempting decomposition that fails

The same country object supplies a birth rate of 11 per thousand and a death rate of 5.7 per thousand, both for 2024. Their difference is 5.30 per thousand, or 0.53% of an exposure population. That is a natural-change rate for the stated source period, excluding migration. It should not be renamed the 2025 population growth rate.

The bundled net migration count, -258,205, refers to 2025. Combining it with those 2024 vital rates and a 2025 population would mix years and stock-versus-exposure concepts. Even if the answer looked close to 359,895, closeness would not validate the decomposition. If it differed, the residual would not automatically represent undocumented migration, data manipulation or an unexplained demographic event.

What a defensible explanation needs

To reconcile change, obtain births, deaths and net migration for one consistent interval and territorial definition, check their coverage, and identify statistical adjustments. If only annual rates are available, explain the exposure population used to estimate counts and label the result an approximation. Keep the original rate, units and reference year visible beside every derived value so another reader can reverse the calculation.

The sixty-second tool helps readers picture the scale of flows by spreading annual rates across time. It cannot tell you which observed events caused the historical change in Türkiye. Use its source labels to ask a better question: are these rates from the same year as the population? When they are not, a concise caveat is more honest than forcing all of the counters to add up.

Try the reasoning

What percentage corresponds to 5.30 per thousand, and why can it not be subtracted from the 2025 growth rate to estimate migration here?

Reveal the explanation

Divide by ten: 5.30 per thousand is 0.53%. But those vital rates refer to 2024, while the growth indicator refers to 2025. The interval and exposure are not aligned. A difference between the rates would not be a verified migration estimate. Even with aligned years, a logarithmic growth rate must not be treated as an additive births-minus-deaths accounting fraction without a consistent rate convention.

The takeaway

Reproduce the stock change first. Explain its components only after matching periods, geography, units and statistical adjustments.
