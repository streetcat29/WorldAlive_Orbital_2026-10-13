The complete reading list

  1. What a live population counter actually measures

    A moving digit is a calculation, not a newly registered person. Reproduce the model before trusting its apparent precision.

  2. Sydney and Istanbul: which population are you comparing?

    A place name is not a boundary. Use the bundled city records to distinguish urban centres, provinces and settlement figures.

  3. Population growth is not births minus deaths alone

    Separate stocks, rates and flows before explaining a change. Türkiye's local records show why a plausible calculation may still be the wrong accounting exercise.

  4. Projections, forecasts and the problem of mixed vintages

    Two values labelled 2025 can disagree without either being a live count. Keep the target year separate from the release that produced it.

  5. An older population does not predict your lifespan

    Japan's age shares and life expectancy answer different questions. Read them without turning a population statistic into a personal countdown.

  6. How to read population rankings without inventing a story

    Choose a common year and definition, inspect the denominator, and separate a numerical ordering from a causal explanation.

A reason to return, not a manufactured update

After exploring a new place, bring back one number and write its source, reference year, geographic definition and denominator. Try another guide when one of those fields changes. There is no streak, popularity claim or automatically renewed publication date.

This is the first six-guide review, not evidence of a long publication history. Calculations are reproducible against the bundled files. The examples remain labelled snapshots until an explicit editorial revision checks both the data and the prose. Corrections should identify the guide, the disputed sentence and the relevant source.
