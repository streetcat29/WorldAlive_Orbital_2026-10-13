Iraq has 7% more people than Afghanistan: 47,020,774 vs 43,844,111, a gap of 3.2 million. Afghanistan is growing faster (+2.77% vs +2.10% a year), adding about 1,213,252 people a year against +989,089. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Afghanistan would overtake Iraq in about 11 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2036) — a projection, not a forecast. Iraq is the older society: 3.4% aged 65+ against 2.4% in Afghanistan, while Afghanistan has more children (42.6% under 15 vs 36.0%). Iraq is 1.6× as densely populated (103.8 vs 63.6 people per km²). People in Iraq live 6.1 years longer on average (72.4 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 13.0× higher in Iraq ($5,410 vs $417).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Afghanistan) and 2025 (Iraq); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
