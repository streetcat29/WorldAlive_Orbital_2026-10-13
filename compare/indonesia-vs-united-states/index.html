United States has 1.2× the population of Indonesia: 341,784,857 vs 285,721,236, a gap of 56.1 million. Indonesia is growing faster (+0.78% vs +0.52% a year), adding about 2,242,079 people a year against +1,785,717. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Indonesia would overtake United States in about 68 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2093) — a projection, not a forecast. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 7.5% in Indonesia, while Indonesia has more children (24.2% under 15 vs 17.1%). Indonesia is 4.0× as densely populated (148.6 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in United States live 7.6 years longer on average (78.9 vs 71.3). GDP per capita is 17.8× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $5,060).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Indonesia) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
