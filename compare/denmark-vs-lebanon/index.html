Denmark has about the same population as Lebanon: 6,009,169 vs 5,849,421, a gap of 159,748. Lebanon is growing faster (+0.75% vs +0.54% a year), adding about 43,621 people a year against +32,263. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Lebanon would overtake Denmark in about 13 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2038) — a projection, not a forecast. Denmark is the older society: 21.1% aged 65+ against 10.4% in Lebanon, while Lebanon has more children (25.6% under 15 vs 15.6%). Lebanon is 3.8× as densely populated (564.4 vs 148.7 people per km²). People in Denmark live 4.3 years longer on average (82.3 vs 77.9). GDP per capita is 17.2× higher in Denmark ($76,970 vs $4,473).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Denmark) and 2025 (Lebanon); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
