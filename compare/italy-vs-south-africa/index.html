South Africa has 10% more people than Italy: 64,747,319 vs 58,915,656, a gap of 5.8 million. South Africa is growing faster (+1.15% vs −0.06% a year), adding about 744,395 people a year against −37,036. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is widening. Italy is the older society: 25.1% aged 65+ against 6.9% in South Africa, while South Africa has more children (25.7% under 15 vs 11.7%). Italy is 3.8× as densely populated (199.5 vs 52.1 people per km²). People in Italy live 17.6 years longer on average (84.0 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 6.6× higher in Italy ($43,309 vs $6,598).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Italy) and 2025 (South Africa); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
