Germany has 1.2× the population of France: 83,491,249 vs 68,720,337, a gap of 14.8 million. France is growing faster (+0.25% vs −0.03% a year), adding about 168,891 people a year against −25,340. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, France would overtake Germany in about 71 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2096) — a projection, not a forecast. Germany is the older society: 23.7% aged 65+ against 22.5% in France, while France has more children (16.2% under 15 vs 13.9%). Germany is 1.9× as densely populated (238.4 vs 126.9 people per km²). People in France live 2.2 years longer on average (83.0 vs 80.8).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (France) and 2025 (Germany); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
