United States has 1.6× the population of Brazil: 341,784,857 vs 212,812,405, a gap of 129.0 million. United States is growing faster (+0.52% vs +0.38% a year), adding about 1,785,717 people a year against +815,392. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is widening. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 11.5% in Brazil, while Brazil has more children (19.4% under 15 vs 17.1%). United States is 1.5× as densely populated (36.8 vs 25.3 people per km²). People in United States live 2.9 years longer on average (78.9 vs 76.0). GDP per capita is 8.4× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $10,713).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Brazil) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
