United States has 2.6× the population of Mexico: 341,784,857 vs 131,946,900, a gap of 209.8 million. Mexico is growing faster (+0.83% vs +0.52% a year), adding about 1,090,386 people a year against +1,785,717. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is narrowing, but too slowly to close within two centuries. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 8.5% in Mexico, while Mexico has more children (24.1% under 15 vs 17.1%). Mexico is 1.8× as densely populated (66.7 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in United States live 3.6 years longer on average (78.9 vs 75.3). GDP per capita is 6.5× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $13,889).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Mexico) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
