Bahamas has about the same population as Iceland: 403,033 vs 392,404, a gap of 10,629. Iceland is growing faster (+1.51% vs +0.44% a year), adding about 5,943 people a year against +1,754. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Iceland would overtake Bahamas in about 2 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2027) — a projection, not a forecast. Iceland is the older society: 16.0% aged 65+ against 12.1% in Bahamas, while Bahamas has more children (17.5% under 15 vs 17.5%). Bahamas is 10.4× as densely populated (39.9 vs 3.8 people per km²). People in Iceland live 8.1 years longer on average (82.8 vs 74.7). GDP per capita is 2.5× higher in Iceland ($98,323 vs $39,455).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Bahamas) and 2025 (Iceland); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
