United States has 4.1× the population of Germany: 341,784,857 vs 83,491,249, a gap of 258.3 million. United States is growing faster (+0.52% vs −0.03% a year), adding about 1,785,717 people a year against −25,340. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is widening. Germany is the older society: 23.7% aged 65+ against 18.4% in United States, while United States has more children (17.1% under 15 vs 13.9%). Germany is 6.5× as densely populated (238.4 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in Germany live 1.9 years longer on average (80.8 vs 78.9). GDP per capita is 1.5× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $60,496).
