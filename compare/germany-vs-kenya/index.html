Germany has 1.5× the population of Kenya: 83,491,249 vs 57,532,493, a gap of 26.0 million. Kenya is growing faster (+1.93% vs −0.03% a year), adding about 1,110,192 people a year against −25,340. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Kenya would overtake Germany in about 19 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2044) — a projection, not a forecast. Germany is the older society: 23.7% aged 65+ against 3.0% in Kenya, while Kenya has more children (36.3% under 15 vs 13.9%). Germany is 2.5× as densely populated (238.4 vs 95.3 people per km²). People in Germany live 17.0 years longer on average (80.8 vs 63.8). GDP per capita is 25.6× higher in Germany ($60,496 vs $2,363).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Germany) and 2025 (Kenya); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
