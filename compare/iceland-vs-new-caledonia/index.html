Iceland has 1.3× the population of New Caledonia: 392,404 vs 295,333, a gap of 97,071. Iceland is growing faster (+1.51% vs +0.92% a year), adding about 5,943 people a year against +2,706. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is widening. Iceland is the older society: 16.0% aged 65+ against 11.8% in New Caledonia, while New Caledonia has more children (21.1% under 15 vs 17.5%). New Caledonia is 4.1× as densely populated (15.9 vs 3.8 people per km²). People in Iceland live 3.9 years longer on average (82.8 vs 78.9). GDP per capita is 3.4× higher in Iceland ($98,323 vs $29,213).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Iceland) and 2025 (New Caledonia); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
