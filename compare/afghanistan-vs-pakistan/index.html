Pakistan has 5.8× the population of Afghanistan: 255,219,554 vs 43,844,111, a gap of 211.4 million. Afghanistan is growing faster (+2.77% vs +1.56% a year), adding about 1,213,252 people a year against +3,981,282. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Afghanistan would overtake Pakistan in about 146 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2171) — a projection, not a forecast. Pakistan is the older society: 4.4% aged 65+ against 2.4% in Afghanistan, while Afghanistan has more children (42.6% under 15 vs 36.2%). Pakistan is 5.1× as densely populated (321.1 vs 63.6 people per km²). People in Pakistan live 1.5 years longer on average (67.8 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 3.8× higher in Pakistan ($1,596 vs $417).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Afghanistan) and 2025 (Pakistan); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
