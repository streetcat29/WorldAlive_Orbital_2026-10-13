Germany has 1.3× the population of South Africa: 83,491,249 vs 64,747,319, a gap of 18.7 million. South Africa is growing faster (+1.15% vs −0.03% a year), adding about 744,395 people a year against −25,340. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, South Africa would overtake Germany in about 22 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2047) — a projection, not a forecast. Germany is the older society: 23.7% aged 65+ against 6.9% in South Africa, while South Africa has more children (25.7% under 15 vs 13.9%). Germany is 4.6× as densely populated (238.4 vs 52.1 people per km²). People in Germany live 14.5 years longer on average (80.8 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 9.2× higher in Germany ($60,496 vs $6,598).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Germany) and 2025 (South Africa); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
