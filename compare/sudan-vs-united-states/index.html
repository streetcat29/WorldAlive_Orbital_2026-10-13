United States has 6.6× the population of Sudan: 341,784,857 vs 51,662,147, a gap of 290.1 million. Sudan is growing faster (+2.38% vs +0.52% a year), adding about 1,227,656 people a year against +1,785,717. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Sudan would overtake United States in about 102 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2127) — a projection, not a forecast. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 3.4% in Sudan, while Sudan has more children (40.2% under 15 vs 17.1%). United States is 1.4× as densely populated (36.8 vs 26.8 people per km²). People in United States live 12.4 years longer on average (78.9 vs 66.5). GDP per capita is 77.3× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $1,165).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Sudan) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
