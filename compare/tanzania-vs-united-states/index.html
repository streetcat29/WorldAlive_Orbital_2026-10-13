United States has 4.8× the population of Tanzania: 341,784,857 vs 70,545,865, a gap of 271.2 million. Tanzania is growing faster (+2.86% vs +0.52% a year), adding about 2,014,190 people a year against +1,785,717. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Tanzania would overtake United States in about 68 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2093) — a projection, not a forecast. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 3.0% in Tanzania, while Tanzania has more children (42.3% under 15 vs 17.1%). Tanzania is 2.0× as densely populated (75.2 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in United States live 11.7 years longer on average (78.9 vs 67.2). GDP per capita is 68.3× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $1,319).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Tanzania) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
