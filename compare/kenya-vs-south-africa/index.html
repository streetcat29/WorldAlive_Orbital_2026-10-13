South Africa has 13% more people than Kenya: 64,747,319 vs 57,532,493, a gap of 7.2 million. Kenya is growing faster (+1.93% vs +1.15% a year), adding about 1,110,192 people a year against +744,395. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Kenya would overtake South Africa in about 15 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2040) — a projection, not a forecast. South Africa is the older society: 6.9% aged 65+ against 3.0% in Kenya, while Kenya has more children (36.3% under 15 vs 25.7%). Kenya is 1.8× as densely populated (95.3 vs 52.1 people per km²). People in South Africa live 2.5 years longer on average (66.3 vs 63.8). GDP per capita is 2.8× higher in South Africa ($6,598 vs $2,363).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Kenya) and 2025 (South Africa); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
