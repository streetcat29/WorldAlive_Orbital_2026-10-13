Mexico has 2.0× the population of South Africa: 131,946,900 vs 64,747,319, a gap of 67.2 million. South Africa is growing faster (+1.15% vs +0.83% a year), adding about 744,395 people a year against +1,090,386. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is narrowing, but too slowly to close within two centuries. Mexico is the older society: 8.5% aged 65+ against 6.9% in South Africa, while South Africa has more children (25.7% under 15 vs 24.1%). People in Mexico live 9.0 years longer on average (75.3 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 2.1× higher in Mexico ($13,889 vs $6,598).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Mexico) and 2025 (South Africa); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
