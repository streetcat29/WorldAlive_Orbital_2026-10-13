United States has 5.9× the population of Kenya: 341,784,857 vs 57,532,493, a gap of 284.3 million. Kenya is growing faster (+1.93% vs +0.52% a year), adding about 1,110,192 people a year against +1,785,717. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Kenya would overtake United States in about 127 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2152) — a projection, not a forecast. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 3.0% in Kenya, while Kenya has more children (36.3% under 15 vs 17.1%). Kenya is 2.6× as densely populated (95.3 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in United States live 15.1 years longer on average (78.9 vs 63.8). GDP per capita is 38.1× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $2,363).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Kenya) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
