United States has 6.6× the population of South Korea: 341,784,857 vs 51,684,564, a gap of 290.1 million. United States is growing faster (+0.52% vs −0.13% a year), adding about 1,785,717 people a year against −66,458. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is widening. South Korea is the older society: 20.3% aged 65+ against 18.4% in United States, while United States has more children (17.1% under 15 vs 10.2%). South Korea is 14.4× as densely populated (529.8 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in South Korea live 4.7 years longer on average (83.6 vs 78.9). GDP per capita is 2.5× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $36,227).
