Papua New Guinea has 3% more people than Greece: 10,762,817 vs 10,413,962, a gap of 348,855. Papua New Guinea is growing faster (+1.75% vs +0.08% a year), adding about 187,947 people a year against +8,832. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is widening. Greece is the older society: 24.4% aged 65+ against 3.6% in Papua New Guinea, while Papua New Guinea has more children (33.1% under 15 vs 12.9%). Greece is 3.5× as densely populated (80.7 vs 22.9 people per km²). People in Greece live 15.6 years longer on average (81.8 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 8.9× higher in Greece ($26,948 vs $3,020).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Greece) and 2025 (Papua New Guinea); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
