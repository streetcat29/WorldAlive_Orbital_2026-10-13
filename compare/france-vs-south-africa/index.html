France has 6% more people than South Africa: 68,720,337 vs 64,747,319, a gap of 4.0 million. South Africa is growing faster (+1.15% vs +0.25% a year), adding about 744,395 people a year against +168,891. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, South Africa would overtake France in about 7 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2032) — a projection, not a forecast. France is the older society: 22.5% aged 65+ against 6.9% in South Africa, while South Africa has more children (25.7% under 15 vs 16.2%). France is 2.4× as densely populated (126.9 vs 52.1 people per km²). People in France live 16.7 years longer on average (83.0 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 7.4× higher in France ($48,986 vs $6,598).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (France) and 2025 (South Africa); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
