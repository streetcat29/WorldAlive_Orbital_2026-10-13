Russia has 1.7× the population of Germany: 143,513,328 vs 83,491,249, a gap of 60.0 million. Germany is shrinking more slowly (−0.03% vs −0.11% a year), adding about -25,340 people a year against −156,235. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is narrowing, but too slowly to close within two centuries. Germany is the older society: 23.7% aged 65+ against 17.8% in Russia, while Russia has more children (17.0% under 15 vs 13.9%). Germany is 27.1× as densely populated (238.4 vs 8.8 people per km²). People in Germany live 7.4 years longer on average (80.8 vs 73.4). GDP per capita is 3.4× higher in Germany ($60,496 vs $17,547).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Germany) and 2025 (Russia); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
