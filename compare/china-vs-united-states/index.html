China has 4.1× the population of United States: 1,406,585,000 vs 341,784,857, a gap of 1.06 billion. United States is growing faster (+0.52% vs −0.17% a year), adding about 1,785,717 people a year against −2,387,972. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is narrowing, but too slowly to close within two centuries. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 14.9% in China. China is 4.1× as densely populated (150.3 vs 36.8 people per km²). GDP per capita is 6.5× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $13,862).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (China) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
