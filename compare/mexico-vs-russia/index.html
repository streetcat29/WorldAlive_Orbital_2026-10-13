Russia has 9% more people than Mexico: 143,513,328 vs 131,946,900, a gap of 11.6 million. Mexico is growing faster (+0.83% vs −0.11% a year), adding about 1,090,386 people a year against −156,235. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Mexico would overtake Russia in about 9 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2034) — a projection, not a forecast. Russia is the older society: 17.8% aged 65+ against 8.5% in Mexico, while Mexico has more children (24.1% under 15 vs 17.0%). Mexico is 7.6× as densely populated (66.7 vs 8.8 people per km²). People in Mexico live 1.8 years longer on average (75.3 vs 73.4). GDP per capita is 1.3× higher in Russia ($17,547 vs $13,889).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Mexico) and 2025 (Russia); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
