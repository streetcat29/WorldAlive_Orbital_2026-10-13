United States has 5.3× the population of South Africa: 341,784,857 vs 64,747,319, a gap of 277.0 million. South Africa is growing faster (+1.15% vs +0.52% a year), adding about 744,395 people a year against +1,785,717. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is narrowing, but too slowly to close within two centuries. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 6.9% in South Africa, while South Africa has more children (25.7% under 15 vs 17.1%). South Africa is 1.4× as densely populated (52.1 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in United States live 12.6 years longer on average (78.9 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 13.6× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $6,598).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (South Africa) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
