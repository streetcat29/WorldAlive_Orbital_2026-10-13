Japan has 1.9× the population of South Africa: 123,366,734 vs 64,747,319, a gap of 58.6 million. South Africa is growing faster (+1.15% vs −0.49% a year), adding about 744,395 people a year against −607,141. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, South Africa would overtake Japan in about 39 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2064) — a projection, not a forecast. Japan is the older society: 30.0% aged 65+ against 6.9% in South Africa, while South Africa has more children (25.7% under 15 vs 11.2%). Japan is 6.6× as densely populated (341.5 vs 52.1 people per km²). People in Japan live 17.7 years longer on average (84.0 vs 66.3). GDP per capita is 5.4× higher in Japan ($35,951 vs $6,598).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Japan) and 2025 (South Africa); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
