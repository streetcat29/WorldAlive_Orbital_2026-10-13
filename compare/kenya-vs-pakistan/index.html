Pakistan has 4.4× the population of Kenya: 255,219,554 vs 57,532,493, a gap of 197.7 million. Kenya is growing faster (+1.93% vs +1.56% a year), adding about 1,110,192 people a year against +3,981,282. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is narrowing, but too slowly to close within two centuries. Pakistan is the older society: 4.4% aged 65+ against 3.0% in Kenya, while Kenya has more children (36.3% under 15 vs 36.2%). Pakistan is 3.4× as densely populated (321.1 vs 95.3 people per km²). People in Pakistan live 4.0 years longer on average (67.8 vs 63.8). GDP per capita is 1.5× higher in Kenya ($2,363 vs $1,596).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Kenya) and 2025 (Pakistan); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
