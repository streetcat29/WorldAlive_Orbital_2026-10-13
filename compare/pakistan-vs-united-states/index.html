United States has 1.3× the population of Pakistan: 341,784,857 vs 255,219,554, a gap of 86.6 million. Pakistan is growing faster (+1.56% vs +0.52% a year), adding about 3,981,282 people a year against +1,785,717. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Pakistan would overtake United States in about 28 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2053) — a projection, not a forecast. United States is the older society: 18.4% aged 65+ against 4.4% in Pakistan, while Pakistan has more children (36.2% under 15 vs 17.1%). Pakistan is 8.7× as densely populated (321.1 vs 36.8 people per km²). People in United States live 11.1 years longer on average (78.9 vs 67.8). GDP per capita is 56.4× higher in United States ($90,027 vs $1,596).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Pakistan) and 2025 (United States); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
