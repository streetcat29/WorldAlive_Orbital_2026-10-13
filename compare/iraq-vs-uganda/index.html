Uganda has 9% more people than Iraq: 51,384,894 vs 47,020,774, a gap of 4.4 million. Uganda is growing faster (+2.70% vs +2.10% a year), adding about 1,388,391 people a year against +989,089. At these source-year rates the modelled relative population gap is widening. Iraq is the older society: 3.4% aged 65+ against 2.2% in Uganda, while Uganda has more children (43.1% under 15 vs 36.0%). Uganda is 2.3× as densely populated (242.7 vs 103.8 people per km²). People in Iraq live 3.9 years longer on average (72.4 vs 68.5). GDP per capita is 4.5× higher in Iraq ($5,410 vs $1,206).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (Iraq) and 2025 (Uganda); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
