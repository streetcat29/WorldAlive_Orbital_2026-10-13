El Salvador has 4% more people than Singapore: 6,365,503 vs 6,111,175, a gap of 254,328. Singapore is growing faster (+1.22% vs +0.43% a year), adding about 74,771 people a year against +27,369. Using continuous exponential growth from the stated base year, if these rates held, Singapore would overtake El Salvador in about 5 years (from the 2025 population bases, around 2030) — a projection, not a forecast. Singapore is the older society: 14.2% aged 65+ against 8.3% in El Salvador, while El Salvador has more children (24.4% under 15 vs 11.7%). Singapore is 27.1× as densely populated (8,241.8 vs 304.5 people per km²). People in Singapore live 11.0 years longer on average (83.3 vs 72.3). GDP per capita is 17.1× higher in Singapore ($98,814 vs $5,767).

Country/territory population bases: World Bank 2025 (El Salvador) and 2025 (Singapore); growth years 2025 / 2025. Annual source values, not live counts; years may differ. Growth is national, not local.
